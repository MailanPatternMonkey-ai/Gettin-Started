1. New event

2026 RW1, previously designated CERNQ52, was a metre-scale near-Earth object discovered on 6 September 2026. ESA Operations reports that it struck the ocean northwest of Australia and that ESA's Meerkat system forecast the impact roughly six hours in advance.

The published orbital solution identifies an Aten-type orbit with approximately a=0.944 AU, e=0.182, i=1.4^\circ, and essentially zero Earth MOID.

2. Directional comparison

A working reconstruction from the pre-impact orbit places the incoming geocentric direction near RA 2^\circ, Dec -8^\circ. Treat this as an analysis-derived reconstruction, not an independently published fitted fireball radiant.

For September 5–6, the American Meteor Society places the Anthelion radiant at RA 356^\circ, Dec -2^\circ, with a typical atmospheric entry velocity of about 30 km/s.

The reconstructed RW1 direction lies approximately 8° from the Anthelion center. That angular separation falls inside the HED watch's stated 10–20° typical radiant uncertainty.

However, direction alone is an insufficient state description. RW1's reconstructed encounter speed is substantially lower than the Anthelion reference speed. The coarse directional match therefore does not establish Anthelion membership or common-source identity.

3. Geometry-Maximization quotient result

When the comparison was changed to an Earth-encounter state retaining geocentric speed, radiant geometry, and encounter timing, their similarity weakened sharply, to approximately D_N\approx0.66.
