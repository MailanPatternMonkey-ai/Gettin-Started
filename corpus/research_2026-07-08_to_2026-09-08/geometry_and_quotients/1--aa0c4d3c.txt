1. System definition
Represent the six entities as discrete information packets:
[X={X_1,X_2,X_3,X_4,X_5,X_6}]
Let each packet contain (I) bits of independently recoverable state information under a fixed coarse-graining. For statistically independent, equally complex packets:
[H(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{6}H(X_i)=6I\ \text{bits}]
Here, (H) is Shannon entropy: the expected information required to specify a state drawn from a defined probability distribution. Information content therefore depends on the encoding, state space, and probability model—not merely on packet count. (Harvard Mathematics Department)
Define two transition branches:
[B_1:\quad X_1\text{ becomes inaccessible; }X_2,\ldots,X_6\text{ remain active}]
[B_5:\quad X_2,\ldots,X_6\text{ become inaccessible; }X_1\text{ remains active}]

2. Raw information-loss calculation
The rigorous loss measure is conditional entropy:
[L_1=H(X_1\mid X_2,\ldots,X_6)]
[L_5=H(X_2,\ldots,X_6\mid X_1)]
Under the equal-and-independent assumption:
[L_1=I]
[L_5=5I]
Therefore:
[\frac{L_5}{L_1}=5]
and
[\Delta L=L_5-L_1=4I]
The one-loss branch eliminates one-fifth as much encoded information as the five-loss branch.
Quantity	One-loss branch	Five-loss branch
Data erased	(I) bits	(5I) bits
Data retained	(5I) bits	(I) bits
Retention fraction	(5/6=83.33%)	(1/6=16.67%)
Relative erasure cost	(1)	(5)

3. Structural-information model
Packet information alone excludes relationships between packets. Introduce (J) bits of structural or mutual information for every functioning pairwise connection.
A network of (n) packets contains:
[\binom{n}{2}]
possible pairwise channels. Define retained structural information as:
[R(n)=nI+\binom{n}{2}J]
Initially:
[R(6)=6I+15J]
One-loss branch
Five packets remain, with ten possible pairwise channels:
[R_1=R(5)=5I+10J]
Structural cost:
[C_1=R(6)-R(5)=I+5J]
Five-loss branch
One packet remains, with no pairwise channels:
[R_5=R(1)=I]
Structural cost:
[C_5=R(6)-R(1)=5I+15J]
The difference is:
[C_5-C_1=4I+10J]
Thus the one-loss branch preserves:
- (4I) additional bits of packet-local information.
- Ten additional pairwise information channels.
- (4I+10J) more total modeled structure.
A normalized stability coefficient can be written as:
[\sigma_1=\frac{5I+10J}{6I+15J}]
[\sigma_5=\frac{I}{6I+15J}]
For (J=0), these reduce to (5/6) and (1/6). Positive (J) increases the structural advantage of retaining the five-packet network.

4. Thermodynamic erasure cost
For irreversible erasure of one bit at temperature (T), the Landauer lower bound is:
[q_{\min}=k_BT\ln 2]
Erasing (mI) bits therefore requires minimum heat dissipation:
[Q_{\min}(m)=mI,k_BT\ln 2]
and corresponding entropy transfer:
[\Delta S_{\min}(m)=mI,k_B\ln 2]
This is a theoretical lower bound for logical erasure, not the total mechanical energy released by a macroscopic transition. (WorryDream)
At (T=300,\mathrm K):
[k_BT\ln2\approx2.871\times10^{-21}\ \mathrm{J/bit}]
Therefore:
[Q_1\geq2.871\times10^{-21}I\ \mathrm J]
[Q_5\geq1.4355\times10^{-20}I\ \mathrm J]
Difference:
[Q_5-Q_1\geq1.1484\times10^{-20}I\ \mathrm J]
The five-loss branch has five times the minimum information-erasure heat and produces four additional units of (I k_B\ln2) entropy relative to the one-loss branch.

5. Closed-system correction
The one-loss branch is not a reduction in the total thermodynamic entropy of the closed system.
For the complete closed system:
[\Delta S_{\text{total}}\geq0]
A decrease in the accessible entropy of the organized packet subsystem must be balanced by entropy appearing in other degrees of freedom:
[
\Delta S_{\text{total}}
\Delta S_{\text{accessible}}+\Delta S_{\text{dispersed}}\geq0]
Logical information that becomes inaccessible is converted into dispersed thermal, mechanical, and correlational information. Logical irreversibility therefore corresponds to entropy generation elsewhere in the full system. (Georgia Tech Sites)
This produces a precise distinction:
[\text{Lower active-subsystem entropy}\neq\text{lower information loss}]
The five-loss branch leaves only one active packet and therefore has fewer remaining degrees of freedom. Its surviving active subsystem has lower Shannon entropy:
[H_{\text{remaining},5}=I]
The one-loss branch leaves five active packets:
[H_{\text{remaining},1}=5I]
Consequently, the one-loss branch does not minimize the Shannon entropy of the surviving subsystem. It maximizes retained information while minimizing entropy production caused by irreversible erasure.
Structural result
Under equal packet size, independence, and equal network coupling:
[\boxed{L_1=I,\qquad L_5=5I}]
[\boxed{C_1=I+5J,\qquad C_5=5I+15J}]
[\boxed{Q_5/Q_1=5}]
The one-loss branch is therefore the minimum-loss transition. It preserves five times as much packet-local data, retains ten pairwise channels rather than zero, and generates one-fifth of the theoretical erasure entropy.
The mathematically accurate description is not “reducing total system entropy.” It is:
[\boxed{\text{minimizing irreversible information dispersal while maximizing retained network structure}}]

The clean answer: (E(t)=E_0e^{-\lambda t}) alone cannot distinguish infrastructure failure from relevance decay. Here, (\lambda) is only an aggregate decay parameter.
The exact loss functions are:
[L_A(t)=I+5E(t)+P_A(t)]
[L_B(t)=5I+15E(t)+P_B(t)]
Removing one node from (K_6) destroys its five incident edges and leaves the ten edges of (K_5). Removing five nodes destroys all fifteen edges. Therefore,
[L_B-L_A=4I+10E_0e^{-\lambda t}-\Delta P(t),\qquad\Delta P=P_A-P_B.]
Route A is selected when this quantity is positive.
For constant (\Delta P), finite crossover occurs only when
[4I<\Delta P<4I+10E_0.]
Then:
[
t_{\mathrm{crit}}
\frac{1}{\lambda}\ln\left(\frac{10E_0}{\Delta P-4I}\right).]
The complete regime map is:
- (\Delta P\le 4I): Route A remains preferred for every finite (t).
- (4I<\Delta P<4I+10E_0): one finite transition from A to B.
- (\Delta P\ge4I+10E_0): Route B is preferred from (t=0).
So Route B is not inevitable from edge decay alone. The claim of mathematical predestination requires (\Delta P>4I), or a growing (\Delta P(t)) that eventually crosses the remaining node-value floor.
To identify what is decaying, decompose the effective edge value:
[
E_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)
C(t),Q(t),R(t),]
where:
- (C(t)): channel or infrastructure capacity,
- (Q(t)): probability that protocol permits successful transmission,
- (R(t)): informational relevance or mutual information.
With exponential components,
[C=C_0e^{-\lambda_Ct},\qquadQ=Q_0e^{-\lambda_Qt},\qquadR=R_0e^{-\lambda_Rt},]
giving
[
E_{\mathrm{eff}}(t)
E_0e^{-(\lambda_C+\lambda_Q+\lambda_R)t}.]
Thus:
[\lambda=\lambda_C+\lambda_Q+\lambda_R.]
This also corrects the guardrail interpretation: guardrail growth belongs primarily in (Q(t)) or (P(t)), not automatically in the intrinsic edge-decay constant. Infrastructure can remain intact while protocol permeability collapses; information can remain available while relevance declines; or all three processes can occur simultaneously.
One further precision: this is graph-value decay, not thermodynamic entropy unless microstates and a probability distribution are defined. “Topology loss” and “Shannon-information loss” are mathematically defensible descriptions; “entropy reduction” requires an additional statistical model.
