## Paper and claim

Coleman, Kuklov, and Tsvelik, *Dual view of the \(Z_2\)-Gauged XY Model in 3D*, [Phys. Rev. Lett. **134**, 236001 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1103/nf4j-kbtj); arXiv:[2502.08708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08708).

Thesis: in the nematic / paired-matter phase (their **phase III**), the charge-one XY field stays confined at every finite gauge coupling. Apparent deconfinement on finite lattices is a large confinement radius, not an asymptotic deconfined phase. They contrast this with Bonati–Pelissetto–Vicari, who report a deconfining large-\(K\) line with vector modes visible after stochastic gauge fixing.

Three phases in their language:

| Phase | Matter | Gauge / visons |
|---|---|---|
| I | disordered | disordered |
| II | disordered | visons confined (topological) |
| III | \(\psi^2\) ordered (nematic / pair) | CKT: still charge-one confined |

## Dual variables and the weight you have to reproduce

Dual degrees of freedom:

- integer link currents \(I_\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\)
- plaquette occupations \(M_p\in\{0,1\}\)
- Kirchhoff constraint \(\nabla\cdot I = 0\) (at \(h_6=0\))
- link-parity constraint \(|I_\ell|+M_\ell\) even

Villain current weight:

\[
w_J^{\rm V}(I)=\exp\left(-\frac{I^2}{2J}\right)
\]

Cosine dual weight is the modified Bessel \(\mathcal{I}_{|I|}(J)\). Mixing those two in one chain is a protocol violation.

Winding and stiffness are CKT Eq. 6, which your protocol adopted verbatim:

\[
W_\alpha=\frac1L\sum_{\ell\parallel\alpha}I_\ell,\qquad
\rho_\alpha=\frac{\langle W_\alpha^2\rangle}{L},\qquad
R_W=\langle W_\alpha^2\rangle=L\rho_\alpha.
\]

Odd \(W_\alpha\) would be charge-one winding. Even winding is the \(\psi^2\) sector.

## What they actually plot as “confinement”

- \(C_1\): charge-one correlator of \(\psi\) (gauge-fixed / directed)
- \(C_2\): gauge-invariant \(\langle(\psi^*)^2\psi^2\rangle\)
- \(R_{1,2}\) or \(\widetilde R_{1,2}\): second-moment / gyration size of those correlators

At \(K=0.2\) (Fig. 4) and at the Villain point \(K=0.7\), \(J=0.336\) (Fig. 5):

- \(\widetilde R_1\) **saturates** to a finite \(\xi(J)\)

They read \(\xi\) from the plateau of \(\widetilde R_1/\sqrt{2}\) on \(L>90\), assuming \(C_1\sim e^{-|z|/\xi}\). The protocol’s “visible only on lattices three to four times \(\xi_{\rm conf}\)” is that observation, not a universal theorem. That is why Appendix B freezes

before a Q2 headline is allowed.

Heuristic they attach to the same data: a charge-one pair is parity-odd and must drag an \(M_p\) membrane; membrane free energy grows with \(\kappa\), so

\[
\xi_{\rm conf}\sim e^{2\kappa}
\]

at large gauge coupling. Deep \(t\to 1\) runs are therefore the easiest place to **fake** deconfinement on finite \(L\). The protocol forbids using only that corner for Q2.

## The axial-gauge detail that forced Appendix A

CKT do odd-current updates **along \(z\) only after imposing** \(u_{ij}=1\) on all \(z\)-links. Residual symmetry is a sign flip of \(\psi\) along those lines.

Your protocol refuses to treat “set every \(z\)-link to \(+1\)” as a self-proving periodic ensemble. On \(T^3\) that condition eats a holonomy bit. The fully summed periodic dual then satisfies

\[
Z_{\rm full}=\mathcal{Z}_{000},
\]

so it **cannot** fluctuate in odd mod-two homology. That is why Q2 is defined only in \(Z_{000}\), why the CKT \(z\)-axial code is a **comparator** (`V-CKT-AX-Z`), and why “no odd windings in \(Z_{\rm full}\)” is an ensemble projection, not evidence of confinement.

If a Villain reconstruction does not keep the periodic \(z\)-closure bit and map it to \(h_z\) / \(q_z\), it is a different conditioned ensemble and cannot be sold as canonical Q2.

## Two different lengths are being mixed

CKT write two formulas that are easy to smash together and should not be.

**Membrane / large-\(\kappa\) heuristic** (phase III, \(\kappa\to\infty\)):

\[
1-K\sim e^{-2\kappa},\qquad \xi\sim\frac{1}{1-K}\sim e^{2\kappa}.
\]

A supplemental variant is \(\xi\approx 2\ln J/|\ln K|\). The vortex-fission estimate is different again:

\[
\ln L \gtrsim \frac{4\kappa}{\pi J}.
\]

At the Villain benchmark those *bare* scales are small:

| Point | \(\kappa\) | \(t\) | \(e^{2\kappa}\) | \(1/(1-t)\) | fission \(L\) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | \(0.800\) | \(0.664\) | \(5.0\) | \(3.0\) | \(\sim 20\) |

It is not the whole story, because CKT’s Fig. 5 point is **almost on top of criticality**:

\[
\frac{J-J_c}{J_c}=\frac{0.336-0.3335}{0.3335}\approx 0.75\%.

Your protocol’s ordered-side Q2 point is the same kind of placement:

\[

Three-dimensional XY correlation length goes as \(\xi_{\rm mat}\sim |t|^{-\nu}\) with \(\nu\simeq 0.67\). A one-percent reduced temperature gives \(\xi_{\rm mat}\) of several tens of lattice spacings if the amplitude is \(O(1)\). Charge-one \(C_1\) then decays on whatever is *larger*: the membrane radius or the critical matter length. That is why CKT can write \(\xi\sim e^{2\kappa}\) in the discussion and still refuse to read \(\xi\) before \(L>90\).

Near \(J_c\), “confinement radius” and “ordinary critical \(\xi\)” are not operationally separable on the sizes they ran.
