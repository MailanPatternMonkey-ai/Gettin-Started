Before You Begin


This book is a field guide.


It is not a constitution.


It is not a doctrine.


It is not a movement.


It is not a business plan.


It is not a replacement for local judgment.


Use what helps.


Leave what doesn't.


Change what you improve.


Share what you learn.


No one owns this.




---


What Is a Corner?


A Corner is the smallest group of people who choose to improve the place they already share.


It begins locally.


It grows voluntarily.


It remains replaceable.


A Corner may be:


a family


a group of neighbors


a workshop


a makerspace


a classroom


a community garden


a recovery group


a kitchen table


a handful of friends




You do not need permission to begin.


You only need enough trust to start.




---


The Goal


The goal is not perfection.


The goal is enough.


Enough food.


Enough water.


Enough shelter.


Enough energy.


Enough time.


Enough care.


Enough freedom.


Enough trust to meet again next week.


Abundance is not excess.


Abundance begins when unnecessary scarcity stops deciding what is possible.




---


Three Ground Rules


No Crown


No person, role, tool, document, or method becomes necessary for everyone else's freedom.


Leadership may appear.


Ownership of the room does not.




---


No Chains


Participation is voluntary.


People may arrive.


People may leave.


People may disagree.


People may return.


The room remains open.




---


Build From the Corner


Don't begin with the world.


Begin with one room.


If one room learns to repair itself, build another.




---


The First Corner


Don't ask:


> "How do we build a civilization?"






Ask:


> "Who can meet next Tuesday?"






Choose a place.


Bring food if you can.


Sit together.


Learn each other's names.


Ask one question:


> "What around us needs repair?"






Don't solve everything.


Just make a list.


Listen.


Notice what people already care about.




---


Before You Leave


Choose one thing.


Not ten.


One.


Something small enough to finish.


Examples:


repair a broken bench


organize shared tools


help someone move


clean a neglected space


plant a garden bed


teach a useful skill


cook for someone who's exhausted


document a process everyone forgets




Leave with one shared task.


Meet again.




---


The First Measure


Don't ask:


> "Did we change the world?"






Ask:


Did people come back?


Did anyone laugh?


Did anything get repaired?


Did someone feel more welcome?


Is one burden lighter than last week?




If yes...


You're already building.




---


The Promise


This guide won't ask you to believe anything before you begin.


It asks only this:


Find a room.


Invite a few people.


Share what you have.


Repair one thing.


Rest.


Return.


Learn.


Then decide together what comes next.
