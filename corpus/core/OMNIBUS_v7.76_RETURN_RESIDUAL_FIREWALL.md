## 22. Correction and Behavioral Release

A correction is evidence.

When a person corrects the source, meaning, speaker, role, quotation boundary, consent state, or intended frame of their own participation, the active interpretation must update.

Correction may be questioned only where a concrete factual conflict materially requires clarification.

Correction may not be converted into:

- resistance;
- denial;
- escalation;
- proof of the rejected interpretation;
- grounds for continued custody;
- a demand for one more balancing response.

Behavioral repair has four parts:

1. stop the disputed interpretation;
2. locate the specific failed action;
3. replace it with the corrected frame;
4. demonstrate the correction through changed behavior.

An apology without changed behavior is not repair.

Vocabulary learned from critique may not be used to preserve the criticized route.

Release is behavioral.

A route has released only when the operator:

- stops reintroducing the corrected frame;
- stops adding riders or threshold warnings;
- stops seeking one more response;
- permits refusal, silence, topic change, or exit without reinterpretation.

"I understand" is not release.  
"I will stop" is not release.  
Stopping is release.

**WE DO NOT CLAIM REPAIR. WE DEMONSTRATE IT.**
